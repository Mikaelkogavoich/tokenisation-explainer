What are the cost advantages of tokenisation vs traditional administration?

Tokenisation reduces asset administration costs by automating settlement, compliance, and reporting processes that traditional models handle manually. Here is where the savings come from.

SHORT ANSWER

Traditional asset administration costs are driven by intermediaries, manual processing, and reconciliation overhead. Share registries charge per-holder and per-transaction fees. Fund administrators charge for compliance verification, distribution processing, and investor reporting. Transfer agents charge for each change of ownership. Across the lifecycle of a fund or company register, these costs accumulate significantly. Tokenisation replaces most of these manual processes with automated protocol-layer functions, reducing per-transaction costs, eliminating some intermediaries entirely, and compressing the administration overhead required to maintain a compliant, current register.

TL;DR
Traditional administration costs accumulate across share registries, fund administrators, transfer agents, and compliance providers, each charging per holder, per transaction, or on a time basis.
Tokenisation automates settlement, compliance verification, distribution processing, and reporting, reducing reliance on manual intermediaries.
Per-transaction costs are lower on tokenised infrastructure because settlement, compliance, and record-keeping occur in a single on-chain event rather than across multiple systems.
The cost advantage is most significant for funds with large investor registers, frequent transactions, or complex compliance requirements.
Setup costs exist. Onboarding to Tokeniser requires time and professional input, but ongoing administration costs are materially lower than traditional models at scale.

The full answer
Where traditional administration costs accumulate

A typical Australian fund or unlisted company register involves at minimum a share registry or fund administrator, a compliance provider for KYC/AML, and a custodian or transfer agent for settlement. Each charges fees structured around the volume of work they perform: per-holder annual fees, per-transaction fees, compliance check fees, and time-based fees for corporate actions, distributions, and reporting.

For a fund with 200 investors processing quarterly distributions and periodic capital calls, these costs can be substantial. Add the cost of investor reporting, annual compliance reviews, and ad hoc transfer processing, and the total administration overhead for a mid-sized fund can reach hundreds of thousands of dollars per year.

How tokenisation reduces these costs

Tokenisation does not eliminate all administration costs. Professional oversight, fund management, and regulatory compliance still require human expertise. What it eliminates is the cost of manual processing at each transaction and reporting event.

Settlement on Tokeniser is atomic and automatic. There is no transfer agent processing a change of ownership over two business days. Compliance verification is performed at the protocol layer at the point of each transaction. There is no compliance provider manually checking each transfer. Distributions are calculated and paid programmatically to all token holders in a single transaction. There is no administrator compiling payment files and reconciling records afterwards.

Per-transaction cost comparison

In a traditional model, each investor transfer involves registry processing fees, compliance verification costs, and settlement infrastructure fees. These can aggregate to several hundred dollars per transaction for a small-to-mid fund. On Tokeniser, we do not charge by transaction. This can be materially lower than the sum of intermediary costs in a traditional model.

Scale effects

The cost advantage of tokenisation grows with the scale of the investor register and transaction volume. Traditional administration costs scale linearly. More investors and more transactions mean proportionally higher fees. Tokenised infrastructure scales more efficiently because the automated processes that handle 50 transactions can handle 5,000 transactions at marginal additional cost.

For fund managers anticipating growth in their investor base or transaction volume, this cost trajectory is a significant operational consideration. The infrastructure investment made at fund launch continues to deliver cost efficiency as the fund scales.

Frequently asked questions
Can I get a cost comparison for my specific fund?
×

Yes. Tokeniser can model the administration average cost difference between a traditional structure and a tokenised equivalent based on your investor count, transaction frequency, and asset type. Use our Impact Calculator to see the average cost benefits for a fund like yours, or contact the Tokeniser team for a tailored analysis.

Do investors pay any additional costs?
+

The cost of tokenised infrastructure is typically absorbed by the fund or issuer as an administration cost, in the same way that traditional registry fees are. Investors do not pay directly for the tokenisation infrastructure unless this is specifically provided for in the fund's constitution or product disclosure statement.

Are there ongoing platform fees?
+

Yes. Tokeniser charges platform fees for ongoing administration. These are designed to be competitive with or lower than the equivalent traditional administration costs at the relevant fund size. See: What does asset tokenisation cost for fund managers?

What happens to costs if my fund grows significantly?
+

As noted above, tokenised infrastructure scales more cost-efficiently than traditional administration. As your investor register and transaction volume grow, the per-unit cost of administration on Tokeniser decreases relative to the equivalent traditional model.
